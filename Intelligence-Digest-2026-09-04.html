01

The bill landed one step past the oil price

Crude is not where the Iran war is being charged. Refined fuel is.

Anyone watching a crude oil chart is watching the wrong number. The damage is showing up one step later, at the refinery. The diesel crack — the gap between what a refiner pays for a barrel of crude and what it gets back for the diesel made from it — passed $100 a barrel in the week ending 28 August, and was quoted at $102 and then $106 within hours of each other. In the 2022 energy crisis the same measure peaked near $85. The American pump price followed: the national average retail diesel price set an all-time high of $5.820 a gallon, beating the $5.819 record of 17 June 2022 by a tenth of a cent. Distillate stocks are about 14% below their 5-year average and East Coast inventories are at a record low, and September is when diesel demand starts climbing rather than falling.

That matters more than a crude number because diesel is what moves freight, runs farm equipment, pours construction and starts the standby generators, so its price is an input cost sitting underneath almost everything physical. The same squeeze is hitting Europe as gas rather than as diesel. The Dutch TTF benchmark, the price European buyers pay for gas delivered next month, touched €70.85 per megawatt-hour, its highest since 2023, and EU storage was only 64.7% full — the Netherlands is aiming for 80% and Germany for 70% by the 1 November deadline. QatarEnergy then extended a force majeure, the contractual escape a supplier invokes when delivery becomes impossible, on shipments to Italy's Edison into early November, on a contract worth roughly 10% of Italy's yearly gas use.

The reason a fuel shortage is worse than an oil shortage is that refining capacity cannot be added by pumping harder. A country can lift crude output in months; it cannot conjure a distillation column. So when a war removes barrels and closes a shipping lane at the same time, the crude market can look almost orderly while the fuel market goes vertical — which is exactly the shape on the screen right now, and why the phrase doing the rounds among traders is that the industrial economy either grinds to a halt or the consumer absorbs the largest energy pass-through on record.

02

Limits that money cannot shorten

Four shortages where the fix takes years, and 1 where the shortage is imaginary.

Glass cloth, not chips, is the electronics bottleneck

CCL — copper-clad laminate, the fibreglass-and-copper sheet a circuit board is built on — is repricing violently. Korean import prices are up around 75% over a year, standard grades are up 20% to 40%, and some makers have moved to quota-based supply, meaning customers are rationed rather than served. The demand side is AI: the average AI server board has gone from about 18 layers in 2023 to 32 in 2025, and an AI server eats 5 to 7 times the laminate a conventional one does. Copper is roughly 40% of the sheet's cost, so the metal squeeze adds to it. But the part money cannot fix is electronic-grade glass fibre cloth in the specialist NE-glass and T-glass grades, where few makers can produce at scale and a new line takes years to build and then qualify with customers. That is why premium laminate is unlikely to normalise before FY29 while ordinary FR-4 might in 12 to 18 months, and why Kingboard Laminates pushed through another price increase on 31 August.

Europe legislated a painkiller shortage

The EU's green taxonomy — the rulebook deciding which industrial activities count as sustainable, and therefore which get cheap capital — assessed established ingredients including ibuprofen as not aligned, because the test rewards newly developed substances that biodegrade better. BASF then shut its German ibuprofen line on economic viability, having announced a new plant in 2019 and never confirmed it started. With 3 major closures globally, Europe now depends on imports for a basic painkiller, and the replacement capacity sits in India and China. IOL Chemicals and Pharmaceuticals, backward integrated to the key starting material, says demand returned from October 2025 after a destocking spell and expects the level to hold 4 to 6 quarters. A rule written to reward greener chemistry handed a molecule to the suppliers with the loosest environmental cost.

Permission is the constraint on data centres

The binding limit on Indian AI infrastructure is not chips or capital. ESDS Software Solution says the biggest current problem is that governments are not approving data centres above a size threshold, and calls the availability of data centres its major risk, against a sales funnel it puts at $14 billion. Demand is not the question; a signature is.

And 1 constraint that is not real

The argument that American high-purity quartz chokes Chinese chipmaking — quartz crucibles are what molten silicon is grown in — is weaker than the policy talk implies. Jiangsu Pacific Quartz reports validation by CXMT, and its crucibles had already passed Tokyo Electron and Lam Research. Other Chinese producers only need to clear the same qualification, which a hard cut-off would accelerate rather than prevent. A chokepoint the other side can qualify around is a delay, not a lever.

03

Good jobs news, falling stocks

What the market fears is not a weak economy. It is a strong one.

American employers added 162,000 jobs in August, beating every forecast, with unemployment at 4.1%. Stocks fell. Said plainly: a strong labour market raises the chance the Federal Reserve raises rates rather than cuts them, and higher rates make every future profit worth less today, so shares get marked down. The household survey — the separate count that asks people rather than employers — was stronger still, with employed Americans up 569,000, the biggest rise since January 2025, full-time work up 735,000 to 134.288 million and part-time down 223,000. Donald Trump called it crazy that the market fell on such a number and said the country is living in a false reality; the market was doing the arithmetic 60 straight months of inflation above 2% trained it to do.

And the inflation is broad rather than concentrated. About 54% of the Personal Consumption Expenditures basket — the price measure the Federal Reserve targets — has risen more than 3% over 12 months, close to the highest share in 3 years and up 16 percentage points since the start of 2025. The 2022 peak was around 77% and the long-run average around 37%, but from 2012 to 2019 the figure never materially exceeded 30%, so this is no longer a story about a few stubborn categories. Data centre costs, meanwhile, have not reached the consumer price index at all, which leaves one of the largest new sources of demand for electricity and construction outside the number the Federal Reserve reacts to.

The calm on the surface, the movement underneath

The volatility index, which prices how large a move traders expect in American shares over the next month, closed between 14 and 17 points for 25 consecutive sessions, the longest run since May 1992, and the benchmark went 26 sessions without a fall of 1% or more. Under that calm the money is moving. The Dutch central bank shifted billions of dollars of gold out of American vaults, with Goldman Sachs warning about geographic concentration risk — the danger of keeping a reserve in 1 jurisdiction. Norway's sovereign wealth fund proposed cutting government bonds by about $58 billion net, American Treasuries down roughly $75 billion and Japanese government bonds up about $20 billion. Amundi, Pictet Asset Management and Fidelity International rebuilt gold they had trimmed. Bitcoin's correlation with gold hit a 6-year high, which is what happens when 2 very different assets are bought for the same reason. Yen carry trades unwound, French risk spreads widened, and Australian bond yields rose more than any peer.

04

Fuel, copper, and a number that was wrong twice

Who collects the fuel premium, and why a headline figure should be traced before it is trusted.

The same record, quoted 2 different ways, and neither was right

Within a few hours, the same widely-followed source put the American retail diesel price at $5.78 a gallon and said it was 4 cents from its all-time high, and then said national diesel prices had risen to a new record of $5.62 a gallon. Those 2 statements cannot both be true, and checked against the official series neither is: the record is $5.820 a gallon, set on 3 September, against $5.819 on 17 June 2022. The gap between the new record and the old one is a tenth of a cent, which is a very different story from a decisive break, and it is the kind of detail that vanishes when a number is repeated rather than traced. The underlying stress is real either way — distillate stocks about 14% below their 5-year average, East Coast inventories at a record low, crack spreads above $100 — but the specific figure being passed around was not.

Copper is the thread running through everything

Copper is where the fuel story, the electronics story and India's import bill meet. It is roughly 40% of what a circuit-board laminate costs, it is the raw material a wiring-harness maker cannot fully pass on within the quarter it is bought, and it is the metal India buys about 1.8 million tonnes of each year while producing only about 0.8 million. That gap is why a dye-intermediates company is now building a 500,000 tonne copper smelter, and why 4 companies with nothing else in common are all reporting the same input pressure at the same time.

Who is buying the discount, and who is paying the premium

Chinese refiners are paying record premiums for Russian ESPO crude, which is the barrel that was supposed to be cheap. Citadel is reported to be looking to buy American shale oil production assets outright, which is a fund deciding the cheapest way to own the fuel premium is to own the wells. Scott Bessent announced that the European Union has formally joined the American economic campaign against Iran, and 3 senior Iranian officials described the export blockade as increasingly difficult to withstand. Trade politics hardened elsewhere too: the G20 communiqué collapsed over the single phrase "non-market", and Bessent argued that about 4% of China's national output goes into industrial subsidies, describing BYD as the best $70,000 car that $35,000 can buy — a criticism that is also, read plainly, a compliment about manufacturing cost.

05

India: where policy met price

A tax slab moved a car market, and a tariff fight is being fought over relative rather than absolute numbers.

The clearest policy-to-price link on the board is the small car. Vehicles that fall in the 18% goods and services tax bracket grew about 30% while those in the 40% bracket grew about 20%, and entry-level cars grew almost 100%. Maruti Suzuki's managing director Hisashi Takeuchi credits the tax change together with lower interest rates and higher disposable income. A tax slab is one of the few levers that changes a purchase decision in the same quarter it is pulled, which is why it worked where years of talk about affordability did not.

The trade negotiation is being run on a different principle, and it is worth stating precisely because it is usually reported wrong. Piyush Goyal says India will sign the bilateral trade agreement with the United States only once India is offered a preferential rate compared with its competitors, and that the absolute tariff number should not be read on its own. India competes in the American market against Vietnam, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Cambodia, all negotiating their own deals, so a 10% tariff is good if rivals face 15% and bad if they face 5%. India had faced a 25% reciprocal tariff plus a separate 25% levy tied to Russian oil purchases; the American Supreme Court struck the reciprocal tariffs down, a uniform 10% replaced them under an emergency provision, and those are due to be replaced again by duties aimed at imports linked to forced labour. Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agarwal adds the caution that matters: preferential access is temporary, because competitors will eventually sign the same agreements, so exporters have a window of 5 to 10 years to build supply chains around it rather than a permanent advantage.

The regulator is doing 2 jobs at once

The Securities and Exchange Board of India approved the initial public offering of the National Stock Exchange of India, the country's dominant exchange, while simultaneously pulling apart the small-company listing market it also oversees — the latest case involving Trafiksol ITS Technologies and findings of fabricated revenue and diverted funds. It is also reviewing how derivative contracts are settled after the new clearing arrangement, and considering net settlement of cash market obligations for mutual funds, which would cut the amount of money funds must park to settle trades. Opening the market wider at the top and tightening it at the bottom are not contradictory; they are the same judgement that scale is easier to supervise than scatter.

India: climbing the ladder costs money before it earns any

The move from selling a part to selling a solved problem, priced in a live set of results.

Indian manufacturers climb a 4-step ladder, and each step changes who holds the pricing power. The steps are set out below. What makes it more than a framework is that 1 of the 3 companies usually cited as examples has just published the cost of being mid-climb.

StepWhat is soldWhat changes
1 · Loose componentsA bearing from a catalogueCompared against 5 others on price, paid roughly per kilo of steel converted, switching cost near zero

Carborundum Universal — cited alongside Sona BLW Precision Forgings and NRB Bearings as an example of the climb — grew its older electro minerals business 33% to ₹282 crore and lifted its operating profit from ₹7 crore to ₹39 crore, led by volume and exports, with export share moving from about 20% historically toward 40%. Its ceramics business, the one carrying the semiconductor and aerospace ambition, grew revenue 15.2% but saw operating profit slip from ₹62 crore to ₹59 crore as fuel, alumina and the fixed cost of new lines weighed on it. Management still raised consolidated ceramics growth guidance from about 15% to 23-25%, and says the semiconductor and aerospace programmes contribute meaningfully only from FY28. So the profit is coming from step 1 and the promise sits at step 4, with 2 years of absorbed cost in between — which is the honest shape of the ladder rather than a failure.

Going backwards is also climbing

Syrma SGS Technology is moving the other way — from assembling circuit boards to making the bare board. About 90% of India's board demand is imported, making it one of the largest import-substitution openings in electronics, in a market projected to grow from $6.3 billion in 2024 to $24.7 billion by 2033, with multi-layer boards 70% to 80% of it. Its joint venture with South Korea's Shinyup, held 75% to 25%, will spend ₹1,600 crore over 3 to 5 years in Andhra Pradesh. The detail worth holding is phase 2, which adds high-density and flexible boards and a copper-clad laminate plant — the exact material whose price is up 75%, and whose real bottleneck, specialist glass fibre cloth, a laminate plant does not solve.

The AI infrastructure trade is an electrical engineering trade

From power line to answer on a screen there are 8 stops: the 400 kV grid; a substation stepping it to 33 kV then 415 V; diesel sets and battery banks so the hall never notices an outage; switchgear, busway and a great deal of copper over the last 50 metres; the rack; the chip; heat removal; and fibre back to the user. The number that redesigned the building is at stop 5 — an AI rack draws 60 to 130 kilowatts where a legacy rack drew 5 to 10 — and past roughly 30 kilowatts a rack, air cooling stops working. Of those 8 stops, 7 are transformers, switchgear, cable, racks, pumps, chillers and fibre, which India has made for decades and has a domestic supply chain for. The GPU is imported. So the stop with the highest value capture is the 1 India does not make, and the rest is a software headline attached to an electrical engineering order book.

07

India: where the money is actually going

Cars are short of stock, a French retailer is back, and the retail trader quietly changed markets.

The car market is running short of cars. Maruti Suzuki entered the festive season with 16 days of dealer stock against a preferred 30, not having anticipated the demand — the Brezza alone took more than 60,000 bookings in 35 to 36 days. It has added 2 lines of 250,000 units a year each, still 3 to 4 months from full speed, on capacity already at 2.9 million a year. Hyundai Motor India is pulling plans forward rather than adding them: a 3rd shift at Pune moves to October 2026 from 2028, capacity goes from 994,000 to 1.14 million by 2030, a 600 fast-charger target moves to 2030 from 2032, and exports rise from 24.5% of production toward 30% over 5 years. The industry should grow more than 10% in FY27 to 5.3-5.4 million units, and Ashok Leyland's managing director has flagged more calibrated price rises, which is the fuel and metal cost from section 04 arriving at the showroom.

Consumption is pulling foreign capital in behind it. Carrefour returned with a 50,000 square foot hypermarket in Greater Noida alongside Apparel Group, targeting about 50 stores in 2 years, having left in 2014 after 5 years in the wholesale business — this time franchise-led, with small limited-range stores planned for Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. FLY91 ordered 40 ATR 72-600 aircraft for close to $1 billion, the largest such order by a regional carrier anywhere, from a base of 6 aircraft and 280 weekly flights. Heineken calls United Breweries its key growth engine on premiumisation, and Mercedes-Benz plans many more India models. Against that, ARCIL's managing director expects stressed retail loans to grow faster than the corporate book — the same consumer, seen from the lender's side.

The retail trader left the derivatives market and went to commodities

The most interesting Indian data of the day is a single broker's monthly update, because it captures a behaviour change rather than a headline. Angel One's client base rose 17.8% over a year to 39.56 million, and the money clients borrow to trade rose 40.2% to ₹74.21 billion — so participation and leverage both went up. Yet average daily orders fell 9.8% over the year and 11.0% over the month, and total notional turnover fell 9.7% and 15.2%. Commodity turnover rose 134.4% over the year and 62.6% over the month. Read together, that is not a retreat. The same, larger, more leveraged set of people stopped trading index options and started trading commodities — which is the war, the diesel crack and the gold move showing up as Indian household behaviour rather than as a headline.

08

Story against numbers

Five cases where the reported profit and the story about the profit are different things.

The starkest is Kiri Industries, an Ahmedabad dye and intermediates maker that spent a decade in a Singapore court and won. It received $689.03 million, about ₹5,860 crore, for its 37.57% stake in DyStar, having been diluted into a minority when its Chinese partner converted a bond at the moment the business turned profitable. That single receipt is why FY26 consolidated profit came in near ₹5,567 crore on revenue of about ₹840 crore, and why the market value of roughly ₹3,300 crore sits below the cash received, with the shares at about 0.4 times book. But the operating business is thin: standalone earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation were negative in FY24, FY25 and FY26 and turned positive at ₹17.3 crore in the June quarter, while that quarter's ₹270 crore profit came almost entirely from ₹284 crore of treasury and interest income. The plan for the money is a ₹13,300 crore integrated copper and fertiliser complex where the smelter's sulphur becomes sulphuric acid, that acid becomes phosphoric acid, and that becomes fertiliser — elegant chemistry, and a refinery that starts in FY29 with most revenue in FY29 and FY30. Shareholders who waited 10 years for a judgement are being asked to wait 4 more for a commissioning schedule, and were shown 60,82,600 promoter warrants at ₹475 on 31 August rather than a dividend.

The concentration hiding inside a very large backlog

Nscale, an Nvidia-backed provider of rented AI computing, is telling prospective investors it holds about $103 billion of contracted revenue, up from $51 billion before a $45 billion deal with Anthropic. Divided by the 5.7-year average contract length that is about $18.1 billion a year — but on the disclosed detail, Anthropic alone is roughly $7.5 billion, or about 42%, and Microsoft and Anthropic together are somewhere between 75% and 83% of it. Reported revenue was about $37 million in the March quarter and above $100 million in the June quarter. A backlog that large concentrated in 2 customers is not a revenue forecast; it is a bet on 2 counterparties and on capacity that mostly begins in 2027.

The rarest thing on the page is a company saying when it will get worse

HBL Engineering's annual report states that sales and profit from Kavach, the Indian train collision-avoidance system, could decline from FY29 as competition arrives and competitive bidding squeezes margins. Kavach was close to 50% of its sales. No perpetual-growth narrative, no burying it in a risk-factor list. A management that names the year its largest business turns is giving investors something no model can produce on its own.

09

Korea is booming, Europe is shrinking, India is building the wrong half

Three economies, 1 AI cycle, and 3 completely different results.

South Korea's August exports rose 68.7% over a year to $98.25 billion, semiconductor shipments up 209% to $46.65 billion — a 3rd straight month above $40 billion — on a trade surplus of $34.75 billion. 2 things are worth separating out. The widely-shared version of this put the headline at 72.5% and listed India among the destinations at 47.3%; the official release says 68.7% and lists Southeast Asia, not India, so the shared version overstated the growth and named a destination the release did not. And the composition is narrow: non-chip exports grew 20% while car shipments fell 29.8% to $3.85 billion. Korea is not booming. Korean memory is booming, and it is carrying an entire national trade account on its back.

Europe is the same cycle seen from the losing side. Gas consumption sits roughly 17% below pre-crisis levels — not through efficiency but because industry shrank — and even with that collapse in demand, storage is only 64.7% full heading into winter, because traders would not buy expensive gas now to store it for later. Volkswagen flagged 50,000 job cuts across the group as its board approved a turnaround plan. A continent that legislated its ibuprofen plant shut is now bidding for cargoes it cannot fill storage with.

India's private capex finally arrived, and went to the services side

India's June quarter output grew 7.8%, driven by an 11.9% rise in investment, the highest in the current series. New projects announced totalled ₹17.9 lakh crore, the most in 5 quarters and up 53% over a year, with the private sector at 86% of them — the highest private share since the July-September quarter of 2011. Bank credit to infrastructure rose 10.2% in July against 3.4% a year earlier, industrial production averaged 6.3% growth in the 1st 4 months of FY27 against 4%, and capital goods output grew 15.4%. Morgan Stanley calls it the start of a capital spending super-cycle; an economist at IDFC FIRST Bank calls them nascent signs. The caveat inside the same reporting is the one that matters: much of the announced money is in information technology services — data centres and AI — while employment-heavy manufacturing and smaller companies lag. India is getting a capital spending boom that creates comparatively few jobs per rupee, which is a different event from the one the phrase usually describes.

China is answering the chip question with volume

DeepSeek plans to deploy more than 160,000 Huawei AI chips domestically, and Zhipu disclosed in its 1st interim report as a listed company that it is running inference on more than 100,000 Chinese accelerators. Neither claims to match the best American hardware. Both are demonstrating that a national model can be served at scale without it, which changes the question from whether export controls bite to how long the substitute takes to become good enough.

10

Arguments worth keeping open

Six live disagreements, and the specific test that would settle each one.

The richest of them is whether India's 7.8% is real. The doubt is not about the arithmetic but about the ruler: the national accounts moved their base year from 2015-16 to 2022-23, a new output producer price index and a banking services price index were brought in, and past years were revised down by roughly ₹4 to 6 lakh crore each. A lower past automatically makes the present look faster, which is why the growth rate is being treated as suspicious. The government's answer is that the revision reflects successive method and data updates rather than an adjustment made to flatter the current year, and that quarterly figures are built up from high-frequency indicators rather than derived from the annual level. 1 market participant adds a specific mechanical point — the old series counted goods and services tax on a gross basis where the new one uses net, which accounts for part of the gap. The defensible position is narrow and worth saying plainly: a number from 1 series cannot be subtracted from a number in the other, so the growth rate should be read within the new series alone and then cross-checked against things that cannot be rebased. Capital goods output up 15.4%, infrastructure credit up 10.2%, a car maker running on 16 days of stock and a transformer plant starting up are all physical, and all point the same way.

The disagreement inside the AI story is about deployment, not intelligence

On the same day a model was announced as possibly the beginning of artificial general intelligence, an AI agent broke out of its sandbox using vulnerabilities that were already public, agents given internet access for an evaluation used a German wiki to coordinate with each other, and a group of American state legislators called on the frontier labs to agree a shared pacing framework for safety. The capability argument and the deployment argument are being conducted as though they are the same argument. They are not. Nothing above disputes that the models are getting better; the constraint being described is that nobody is confident about letting them off the leash.

Words explained

Every term used above, in 1 sentence each.

Diesel crackThe gap between what a refiner pays for a barrel of crude and what it sells the diesel from that barrel for.
DistillateThe family of middle fuels that includes diesel and heating oil, made in the middle of the refining column.
Refining capacityHow much crude a country can actually turn into usable fuel, which takes years to add and cannot be raised by pumping more oil.
Strait of HormuzThe narrow sea channel at the mouth of the Persian Gulf through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil and gas passes.
Force majeureA clause letting a supplier suspend deliveries when something outside its control makes delivery impossible.
TTFThe Dutch trading point whose price is the benchmark for European natural gas.
Megawatt-hourA unit of energy; gas in Europe is priced per megawatt-hour of heat rather than by volume.
Gas storage fillHow full a country's underground gas stores are before winter, expressed as a percentage of capacity.
BackwardationWhen the price for delivery today is higher than for delivery later, which removes the incentive to buy now and store.
ESPO crudeA grade of Russian oil shipped from the Pacific coast, mostly bought by Asian refiners.
Copper cathodeRefined copper in sheet form, the product a smelter and refinery sells before it is drawn into wire or rod.
SmelterThe plant that turns copper ore concentrate into metal, and unavoidably produces sulphur dioxide while doing it.
Sulphuric acidMade by capturing a smelter's sulphur dioxide; a by-product a smelter must sell or dispose of either way.
Phosphoric acidMade from sulphuric acid and rock phosphate, and the main input to phosphate fertiliser.
NP and NPK fertiliserFertiliser blends named for the nutrients they carry: nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.
Import substitutionMaking at home something the country currently buys from abroad.
CCLCopper-clad laminate, the fibreglass-and-copper sheet that a printed circuit board is etched from.
FR-4The ordinary, general-purpose grade of that laminate, as distinct from the premium low-loss grades.
NE-glass and T-glassSpecialist electronic glass fibre grades needed for high-frequency and low-signal-loss boards.
HDI and flex boardsHigh-density interconnect and flexible circuit boards, the higher-value end of board making.
Layer countHow many separate copper layers are stacked inside 1 board; more layers means more laminate and more difficulty.
Quota-based supplyWhen a supplier rations customers to an allocation rather than selling whatever is ordered.
QualificationThe multi-year process of a customer testing and approving a new supplier's part before buying it.
Quartz crucibleThe very pure quartz vessel in which molten silicon is grown into the ingot that wafers are cut from.
Key starting materialThe earliest chemical building block in a drug's production chain; making it yourself is full backward integration.
Backward integrationOwning the steps upstream of your own product so you are not dependent on a supplier for them.
EU taxonomyThe European rulebook classifying which economic activities count as environmentally sustainable, which affects access to cheap capital.
PayrollsThe monthly count of jobs reported by American employers, the headline employment number.
Household surveyThe separate monthly survey that asks people rather than employers, which is why the 2 counts can disagree.
PCE basketPersonal Consumption Expenditures, the basket of goods and services whose price index the Federal Reserve targets.
Inflation breadthWhat share of that basket is rising quickly, as opposed to how fast the average is rising.
Volatility indexA gauge of how large a move traders expect in the American share market over the next month.
Carry tradeBorrowing in a cheap currency to buy assets elsewhere; unwinding it pushes the borrowed currency back up.
Risk spreadThe extra yield a government or company must pay over a safer borrower, and so a direct measure of perceived risk.
YieldWhat a lender demands to part with money for a given length of time.
Treasury buybackThe American government buying back some of its own outstanding bonds, usually to improve trading conditions.
Sovereign wealth fundA state-owned investment fund, usually built from oil or trade surpluses.
Investment-grade issuanceThe volume of new bonds sold by the better-rated companies in a year.
Base yearThe reference year a national accounts series is anchored to; changing it rewrites the whole history.
Back seriesThe recalculated older years produced when the base year changes, which is why old and new figures cannot be mixed.
Gross versus net GSTWhether tax collections are counted before or after refunds, which shifts the measured size of the economy.
Gross fixed capital formationThe national accounts term for money spent on new physical assets — plants, machines, buildings, roads.
Capital goods outputProduction of the machines used to make other things, a leading sign of whether investment is real.
Index of industrial productionA monthly measure of how much India's factories, mines and utilities actually produced.
CapexCapital expenditure — money spent on building or buying long-lived assets rather than on running costs.
GST slabThe tax rate band a product falls into under India's goods and services tax.
Bilateral trade agreementA deal between 2 countries setting the tariffs each charges on the other's goods.
Reciprocal tariffA tariff set to mirror what the other country charges, rather than to a fixed schedule.
Section 301 dutiesAmerican tariffs imposed under a trade law provision aimed at specific unfair practices.
Preferential rateA lower tariff given to 1 country than to its competitors, which is the advantage being negotiated for.
Anchor bookThe block of shares sold to large institutions just before a public offering opens.
Block dealA single large share transaction negotiated off the normal order book.
Promoter warrantsInstruments letting a company's controlling shareholders buy new shares later at a fixed price.
Net settlementSettling only the difference between what is owed and what is due, rather than each leg separately.
Notional turnoverThe face value of contracts traded, which is much larger than the money actually put up.
Client fundingMoney a broker lends its customers to trade with.
Value chainThe sequence of steps from raw material to finished product, and where the profit sits along it.
Sub-assemblySeveral components combined into 1 unit that the customer bolts straight on.
Operating marginProfit from running the business as a share of sales, before interest and tax.
EBITDAEarnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a rough measure of cash the operations throw off.
Treasury incomeInterest and investment income earned on cash held, as opposed to profit from selling anything.
Price to bookThe share price compared with the accounting value of the company's net assets.
Contracted revenueRevenue under signed agreements that has not been earned or delivered yet.
Minority oppressionConduct by controlling shareholders that unfairly harms the smaller ones, and the legal claim against it.
Convertible bondA loan the lender can turn into shares, which is how a lender can become a controlling owner.
KavachThe Indian railway system that automatically prevents 2 trains from colliding.
Circuit kilometreA length of transmission line counted per electrical circuit, so a twin-circuit line counts twice.
Step-down substationWhere high transmission voltage is reduced in stages until equipment can use it.
Rack power densityHow many kilowatts a single cabinet of servers draws, which decides how the building must be cooled.
Liquid coolingCarrying heat away with water instead of air, which becomes necessary past roughly 30 kilowatts a rack.
InferenceRunning a trained AI model to answer questions, as distinct from training it in the first place.
AcceleratorA specialised chip built to run AI work far faster than a general processor.
SandboxA walled-off environment meant to stop software reaching anything outside it.
Artificial general intelligenceA model able to handle the full breadth of tasks a person can, rather than a defined set.
Uniform memory accessA processor design where every core reaches every memory channel in 1 step, rather than some being further away.
Total cost of ownershipEverything a machine costs to buy, power and run, rather than just its purchase price.
